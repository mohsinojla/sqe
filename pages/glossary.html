Glossary

Every defined term used across the five lectures and the ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003 standard. Use Ctrl+F to jump straight to a term.

Baseline

Formally approved version of a configuration item, regardless of media, formally designated and fixed at a specific time during the configuration item's life cycle. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Configuration item

An item or aggregation of hardware, software, or both, designated for configuration management and treated as a single entity in the configuration management process. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Contract review

A pre-project SQA component with two stages — proposal draft review (before submission to the customer) and contract draft review (before signing) — used to catch loosely defined requirements and unrealistic budgets/schedules before they cause quality problems. (Lecture 4)

Correctness

A product operation quality factor covering the output mission, required accuracy, completeness, up-to-dateness, availability, and coding/documentation standards compliance. (Lecture 3 — McCall's model)

COTS (Commercial-Off-The-Shelf)

A product available for purchase and use without the need to conduct development activities. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Efficiency

A product operation quality factor: the hardware resources needed to perform all the functions of the software. (Lecture 3)

Flexibility

A product revision quality factor: the capabilities and effort required to support adaptive maintenance activities. (Lecture 3)

Implementation

The process of translating a design into hardware components, software components, or both. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Integrity

A product operation quality factor dealing with software system security — preventing unauthorized access and restricting read/write access. (Lecture 3)

Interoperability

A product transition quality factor: creating interfaces with other software systems or with other equipment firmware. (Lecture 3)

Life cycle model

A framework of processes and activities concerned with the life cycle, organized into stages, that also acts as a common reference for communication and understanding. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Maintainability

A product revision quality factor: the effort needed by users and maintenance personnel to identify the reasons for failures, correct them, and verify success — supported by modular structure and documentation. (Lecture 3)

PDCA cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act)

A four-stage cycle for managing processes and a quality management system as a whole: Plan (set objectives and resources), Do (implement), Check (monitor and measure against objectives), Act (improve performance). (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Portability

A product transition quality factor: adapting a software system to other environments — different hardware, different operating systems, and so on. (Lecture 3)

Quality assurance (QA)

IEEE: (1) A planned and systematic pattern of all actions necessary to provide adequate confidence that an item or product conforms to established technical requirements; (2) a set of activities designed to evaluate the process by which the products are developed or manufactured — contrast with quality control. Carried out throughout development and maintenance. (Lecture 2)

Quality control (QC)

"A set of activities designed to evaluate the quality of a developed or manufactured product" (IEEE, 1991) — done once production is complete. (Lecture 2)

Quality engineering

Managing customers' varying quality expectations as an engineering problem: selecting and executing appropriate QA activities to meet or exceed expectations while minimizing cost and project risk. Comprises pre-QA, in-QA, and post-QA activities. (Lecture 5 — Jeff Tian)

Quality factor

A content group classifying issues related to software attributes, use and maintenance, as defined in requirements documents. McCall's model defines 11 quality factors across product operation, revision and transition categories. (Lecture 3)

Regression testing

Testing following a modification to a test item or its operational environment, to identify whether regression failures occur. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Reliability

A product operation quality factor dealing with failures to provide service — the maximum allowed software system failure rate. (Lecture 3)

Reusability

A product transition quality factor: using modules originally designed for one project in a new project, saving resources, shortening development, and providing higher-quality modules. (Lecture 3)

Risk-based thinking

Determining the factors that could cause processes and the quality management system to deviate from planned results, putting in place preventive controls to minimize negative effects, and maximizing opportunities as they arise. (ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003)

Software (IEEE definition)

Computer programs, procedures, and possibly associated documentation and data pertaining to the operation of a computer system. (Lecture 2)

Software error

A mistake made by a programmer — a grammatical error in code, or a logical error in carrying out one or more of the client's requirements. (Lecture 2)

Software failure

Disruption of the use of the software caused by triggering ("activating") a certain fault. A fault becomes a failure only once activated. (Lecture 2)

Software fault

A software error that can cause improper functioning of the software in general or in a specific application. (Lecture 2)

Software quality (Pressman's definition)

Conformance to explicitly stated functional and performance requirements, explicitly documented development standards, and implicit characteristics expected of all professionally developed software. (Lecture 2)

Software quality assurance — expanded definition

A systematic, planned set of actions necessary to provide adequate confidence that the software development or maintenance process conforms to established functional technical requirements as well as managerial requirements of keeping schedule and operating within budgetary confines. (Lecture 2)

Testability

A product revision quality factor: special features in the programs that help the tester, e.g. predefined intermediate results, log files, and automatic diagnostics at startup. (Lecture 3)

Usability

A product operation quality factor: the scope of staff resources needed to train a new employee and to operate the software system. (Lecture 3)
