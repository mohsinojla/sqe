Quality Engineering

Different customers expect different things from "quality." Quality engineering treats those expectations as an engineering problem: select and run the right QA activities to meet or exceed them, while minimizing cost and project risk.

The quality engineering process

Measurements must be taken alongside QA activities themselves to confirm quality goals are being met. Both in-process and post-mortem data are analyzed with models to produce an objective quality assessment. The process has three major activity groups:

1. Pre-QA: Quality planning

  • Set specific quality goals
  • Form an overall QA strategy — select QA activities, and choose measurements/models for feedback and assessment

2. In-QA: Execute & handle defects

Execute the planned QA activities. A key part of normal execution is dealing with discovered problems as they surface.

3. Post-QA: Measure, assess, improve

Provide quality assessment and feedback so management decisions can be made and quality-improvement initiatives can be carried out.

Quality planning: goal setting & strategy formation

Setting quality goals

Match customer quality expectations with what can be economically achieved, in three sub-steps:

  1. Identify quality views and attributes meaningful to target customers and users.
  2. Select direct quality measures usable to measure those attributes from the customer's perspective.
  3. Quantify these measures into quality goals, considering the market environment and the cost of achieving different goals.

Forming a QA strategy

Plan for two basic elements:

  1. Map the quality views, attributes and quantitative goals to a specific set of QA alternatives.
  2. Map external direct quality measures into internal indirect ones via selected quality models — choosing indirect measures and models usable for assessment and analysis.
Worked example — inDrive Define a quality roadmap for the core bidding and ride-matching engine using a two-stage quality framework. If product managers insist on adding an AI-driven "suggested fare" feature right before rainstorm season, which QA checks can be streamlined, and which must remain non-negotiable to prevent app crashes?

Cost of different quality levels

Cost splits into two major components: the failure cost and the development cost. Customers care most about total failure cost, Cf, estimated from average single-failure cost cf and failure probability pf over a defined operating duration:

Cf = cf × pf

Failure probability relates to reliability R as pf = 1 − R, where R is the probability of failure-free operation for a specific period and input set.

To minimize Cf, you can lower cf or pf. cf is usually fixed by the nature of the application and environment — hard to change without other cost. Lowering pf (improving reliability) typically means more development cost: additional testing time, extra QA techniques, and so on. An engineering decision must match quantified customer expectations against their willingness to pay.

Factors in forming a QA strategy

Influence of quality perspectives & attributes

Different customers, users and market segments call for different QA alternatives. Usability testing techniques may ensure usability well but do little for functional correctness.

Influence of different quality levels

Quantitative quality levels in the goals affect which QA techniques make sense. Fault-tolerant features cost substantially more than systems without them — relevant for highly dependable or safety-critical systems.

Quality assessment & improvement

Parallel and post-QA activities close the quality engineering loop, enabling decisions such as product release and follow-up improvement initiatives.

1. Measurement

Defect measurements, measurements to track QA activities, and project management measurements feed subsequent analysis and modeling.

2. Analysis & modeling

Fit measurement data to analytical models for a quantitative, objective assessment of current product quality and an accurate prediction of future quality.

3. Feedback & improvement

Results inform project scheduling, resource allocation, and other management decisions.

4. Follow-up

Beyond immediate use, follow-up activities affect long-term quality and organizational performance.

Quality engineering in software processes

Quality engineering is integral to the overall software engineering process, alongside cost and schedule concerns. It spans pre-QA quality planning as well as post-QA measurement/analysis carried out parallel to and after QA activities.

Waterfall-style processes

Pre-QA quality planning is typically carried out during market analysis, requirement gathering and product specification. Project planning — normally about languages, tools and technologies — should expand to include (1) QA strategy choices and (2) measurement /models for monitoring progress and providing feedback.

Incremental / iterative / spiral / XP

Pre-QA activities play an even more active role, repeating at the start of every subpart or iteration: set quality goals per subpart, choose QA activities, techniques, measurements and models. The overall quality goal evolves from these sub-goals iteratively.

In-class exercises

Exercise — priceoye.pk flash sale

Applying the two-stage quality planning model (quality goals + strategy formulation) to an online electronics store:

  1. If an event discount is applied (flash sale + bank card discounts), what issues can occur in real time?
  2. How can these issues be addressed? (Possible answers: don't apply the event discount, or formulate a mitigation strategy that doesn't sacrifice the opportunity cost.)
