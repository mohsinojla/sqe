Components of the Software Quality Assurance System

The seven environmental challenges from Lecture 1 don't fix themselves — they are addressed by six interlocking groups of SQA components, from pre-project contract review all the way to the human organization that runs the whole system.

On this page

  1. The SQA system at a glance
  2. The six components
  3. Contract review, in depth

The SQA system at a glance

The software development and maintenance environment directly shapes what an SQA system needs to do — recall the seven environmental challenges from Lecture 1: contractual conditions, customer–supplier relationship, teamwork, coordination with other software teams, interfaces with other systems, continuity despite team changes, and long-term maintenance.

The six components

1. Pre-project components

  • Contract review — verifying project commitments were adequately defined given the resources, schedule and budget.
  • Development and quality plans — verifying they were correctly determined.

2. Project life-cycle activities assessment

Development life cycle — detecting design and programming errors via:

  1. Reviews
  2. Expert opinions
  3. Software testing

Operation–maintenance phase — applied mainly to functionality-improving maintenance tasks, and to assuring the quality of work performed by subcontractors and other external participants.

3. Infrastructure error prevention & improvement

Eliminating, or at least reducing, the rate of errors based on the organization's accumulated SQA experience.

4. Software quality management

Control of development and maintenance activities, and early managerial support actions that mainly prevent or minimize schedule and budget failures and their outcomes.

5. Standardization, certification & assessment

  • Utilization of international professional knowledge.
  • Improved coordination of the organizational quality system with other organizations.
  • Assessment of quality-system achievements on a common scale.

6. Organizing for SQA — the human components

Initiating and supporting implementation of SQA components, detecting deviations from SQA procedures and methodology, and suggesting improvements.

Users: managers, testing personnel, the SQA unit, and practitioners.

Contract review, in depth

Loosely defined requirements and unrealistic budgets/schedules are expected to yield low-quality software. Unrealistic professional commitments lead to failure to achieve the required quality, and schedule/budget failures are typically accompanied by lower-than- acceptable quality — driven by management pressure "to save time" and "to save resources."

Contract review happens in two stages:

Stage 1 — Proposal draft review

Reviews the final proposal draft and its foundations: customer requirement documents, additional customer details/explanations, cost and resource estimates, and existing contracts or drafts with partners and subcontractors.

Nine objectives (see the full checklist):

  1. Customer requirements clarified and documented
  2. Alternative approaches examined
  3. Formal customer/supplier relationship aspects specified
  4. Development risks identified
  5. Resource & timetable estimates adequately prepared
  6. Firm's capacity to perform the project examined
  7. Customer's capacity to fulfill commitments examined
  8. Partner/subcontractor participation conditions defined
  9. Proprietary rights defined and protected

Stage 2 — Contract draft review

Reviews the contract draft against the proposal and the understandings (including changes) reached during contract negotiation sessions.

Three objectives:

  1. No unclarified issues remain in the contract draft
  2. All post-proposal understandings are correctly documented
  3. No "new" changes, additions, or omissions have entered the contract draft
